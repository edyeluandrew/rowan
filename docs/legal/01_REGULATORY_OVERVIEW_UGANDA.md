ROWAN — REGULATORY OVERVIEW (UGANDA)

Document type: Internal reference

This note maps regulatory themes for Rowan in Uganda. It is not legal advice. Confirm every registration or licence position with counsel and the relevant authority before marketing a public mainnet service.

1. Product description

Rowan, operated by Beta Tech Labs Company Limited, is software that enables users to hold digital assets (including Stellar USDC), transfer them on supported networks, convert to and from local currency through peer-to-peer traders, and purchase utilities such as airtime and data through third-party providers.

At launch the company does not hold itself out as a bank, a deposit-taking institution, or a licensed mobile money operator. Local currency movement may be executed by licensed third parties (mobile network operators, payment service providers, or integration partners). Rowan must not claim third-party licences as its own.

2. Principal themes and bodies (indicative)

Money laundering and terrorist financing. Applicable anti-money laundering legislation and the work of the Financial Intelligence Authority (FIA) inform customer due diligence, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting.

Data protection. The Data Protection and Privacy Act and the Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO) inform privacy notices, lawful basis, security, and breach handling.

Payments and electronic money. Bank of Uganda frameworks applicable to payment systems, electronic money, and agents primarily regulate entities that operate those services. They remain relevant where partners settle mobile money or when Rowan itself operates prefunded fiat.

Tax and company law. Uganda Revenue Authority requirements (including a company TIN) and Uganda Registration Services Bureau formalities apply to the corporate entity.

3. Founder actions

Maintain incorporation records and tax registration.
Appoint a named compliance contact (an interim appointment is acceptable for pilot operations).
Adopt the AML and CFT Policy and keep it aligned with product limits in software.
Obtain counsel advice on Personal Data Protection Office registration timing before a large public launch.
Maintain a written inventory of third-party payment, identity, and utility vendors and their jurisdictions.

4. Pilot and public mainnet

Closed or testnet pilot. Limited invitees, clear labelling of test value, transaction caps, and preferably identity verification at least to BASIC level before cash conversion features.

Public mainnet. Published user terms and privacy policy, live identity verification path, production utility and payment rails, live monitoring and reporting processes, and any reserves or insurance agreed with counsel.

Do not market Rowan as a fully regulated payment institution unless counsel confirms that status.

5. Stack of commercial documents

End users are bound by the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy.
Peer-to-peer traders are bound by the Trader Agreement.
Integration partners are bound by the Partner Integration Agreement and technical schedules.
The AML and CFT Policy is internal and may be shared in redacted form under non-disclosure.

6. Questions for counsel

Whether the current feature set falls within licensed payment-service categories under Bank of Uganda frameworks.
Minimum customer due diligence for crypto-to-mobile-money conversion under Uganda anti-money laundering rules.
Cross-border implications when multi-country corridors return.
Classification of Stellar USDC under investment or securities analysis, if any.
Data residency and international transfer requirements.
